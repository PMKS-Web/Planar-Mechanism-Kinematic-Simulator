One DOF needs one independent input. A count of one alone does not guarantee that the mechanism can animate.

Kutzbach's planar equation

M = 3(N − 1) − 2J₁ − J₂

M is mobility. A free planar body can translate in two directions and rotate, giving three freedoms. Subtracting one body fixes the ground.

A lower pair (pin or slider) permits one relative motion and removes two freedoms. A higher pair permits two relative motions and removes one freedom.

Grübler's lower-pair form

M = 3(N − 1) − 2J₁

PMKS+ represents its connections with pins and sliders, including explicit blocks in slots, so J₂ = 0. For a one-DOF mechanism this becomes 3N − 2J₁ − 4 = 0.

A four-bar example has four bodies including ground and four pin pairs: 3(4 − 1) − 2 × 4 = 1.

  • Count all ground as one body, including fully anchored rails.
  • Welded links and links sharing two or more joints count as one rigid body.
  • A joint joining k distinct bodies contributes k − 1 lower pairs. Three bodies at one pin contribute two pairs.
  • A tracer point on just one body contributes no pair.
  • Count each mechanism separately. An input drives an existing freedom; it is not subtracted from this count.

When geometry changes the answer

The equations assume independent constraints. When the count is below one, PMKS+ checks the constraint geometry.

Instantaneous mobility =
3(N − 1) − rank(A)

A is the constraint Jacobian. PMKS+ then checks whether the candidate motions survive a second-order constraint check. An instantaneous freedom at a tangency may not produce finite motion.

The geometry result replaces the count only if it finds at least one surviving motion. A failed position solve may also check for additional freedoms.
